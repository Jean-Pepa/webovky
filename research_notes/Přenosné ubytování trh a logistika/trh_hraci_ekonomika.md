## 1. Market size and growth (global, Europe, CEE): how reliable are the figures?

### Takeaway
I found no credible published size for the CEE or even the European *rental* market for site cabins and modular space, or for worker camps. The "market size" press releases mostly measure permanent modular construction and are low quality. Defensible anchors come bottom-up from company reports. WillScot, the North American leader, had $2.28 bn revenue in 2025, down 4.8%. Modulaire (Algeco) had €1.42 bn in 2021, the latest hard figure found. The listed workforce-accommodation operators are each below $0.7 bn. The Czech demand that matters is event-driven: September 2024 flood relief, with up to CZK 1 bn for *renting* temporary homes, and construction of the new Dukovany units, with 6,000–10,000 workers at peak from about 2029.

### Inferences
- **Bottom-up size of the identifiable rental pool.** Modular-space and storage rental: WillScot about $2.3 bn, Modulaire probably ≥ €1.5 bn today (€1.42 bn in 2021; unverified since), Portakabin about £0.35–0.4 bn+, CONTAINEX (unknown year; includes sales). Add the mid-caps ATCO and Black Diamond. Listed workforce-accommodation revenue: Civeo $0.63 bn, Target $0.32 bn, plus the camp parts of Black Diamond, ATCO, Dexterra and Red Sea. That is a few billion USD, far below Market Intelo's $14.2 bn, which probably includes captive or in-house camps and services and cannot be validated.
- **Cycle.** North America shrank in 2025: volumes fell and the leader pushed rates up. Its 2026 rebound is tied to events (the World Cup) and large projects. Europe's leader points to defence, energy, infrastructure and the public sector. These match the Czech demand pockets: Dukovany, defence and flood relief.
- **For a Czech startup,** the concrete, datable demand pockets are more decision-relevant than any global CAGR: a flood-relief rental budget of up to CZK 1 bn, and 6,000–10,000 Dukovany workers from 2029 to the mid-2030s, against only about 23% of the region's 7,500 beds being near the plant.

## 2. Key players and their scale (fleet, revenue, regions, utilisation)

### Takeaway
Modular-space rental is led by WillScot in North America (about $2.3 bn revenue, about 250 branches) and Modulaire/Algeco in Europe and APAC (€1.42 bn in 2021; 250,000–290,000 units claimed). Behind them are Portakabin (about £0.4 bn) and the manufacturer-renters CONTAINEX (about 30,000–40,000 own units, about 120,000 with dealers) and ELA (60,000+ units), plus the Canadian mid-caps ATCO (about 27,000 units) and Black Diamond. Cadolto and Kleusberg are mainly permanent-modular builders. General equipment-rental firms rent site cabins only as a side line. Workforce accommodation is led by Civeo (about 26,500 rooms, $631 m), Target Hospitality (about 16,600 beds implied, $321 m), Black Diamond WFS (13,514 rooms), ATCO, Dexterra and Red Sea International. Earnings in this segment swing hard with single contracts and commodity cycles.

### Inferences
- **Two tiers.** (a) A few consolidators with very large fleets, now owned by infrastructure or PE investors at infrastructure-like valuations: Modulaire at about $5 bn (Brookfield), Portakabin at >£1.5 bn (Antin), WillScot listed. (b) Manufacturer-renters with dealer networks (CONTAINEX, ELA, KOMA) and many local renters.
- **Rate benchmarks.** Monthly rent per unit is about $1,243 at WillScot (≈ CZK 27,000), CAD 896–910 at ATCO and Black Diamond (≈ CZK 14,500), against CZK 3,100–4,800 for a Czech obytný kontejner (section 4). North American rents are roughly 3–9× Czech rents. The comparison is distorted: units are larger and rents bundle VAPS such as furniture, steps and A/C.
- **Earnings volatility.** Workforce-camp operators depend on single sectors or contracts. Target's adjusted EBITDA fell 73% on government contract endings, and Civeo's Canadian billed rooms fell about 30% on oil-sands cuts. New demand themes are data centres, defence and large projects.
- **Utilisation targets.** Even "optimal" MSS utilisation is around 78% (Black Diamond), and the US leader runs at about 52–60%. Fleets carry large idle capacity by design, and some of it sits in the wrong places.

## 4. Business models and concrete prices: rent, purchase, delivery/HDS, deposits, minimum periods, bed-night comparison

### Takeaway
Czech rental of a 6 m obytný kontejner costs about CZK 3,100–4,800 per month excl. VAT, tiered by duration. Minimum terms run 1–2 months and deposits are about CZK 10,000. Transport is charged separately at about CZK 35–55/km, often counted both ways, plus CZK 500–1,600 per move for crane work, handling and cleaning. A new 6 m unit sells for about CZK 110,000–155,000 and a used one for about CZK 60,000–125,000; an equipped residential container starts around CZK 362,000 incl. VAT. Full-service camp bed-nights cost $71–100 at Target and Civeo (≈ CZK 1,560–2,200), against CZK 135–600 per night or CZK 4,800–9,000 per month for a bed in a Czech worker ubytovna, a 4–16× gap.

- **Bed-night gap.** A Civeo bed at about $97–100 per night is ≈ CZK 2,100–2,200, about CZK 64,000–66,000 per month. Target at about $71 is ≈ CZK 1,560. A Czech ubytovna costs CZK 135–600 per night or CZK 4,800–9,000 per month, roughly 4–16× cheaper. The Czech worker-housing market is low-price and low-service. Full-service camp pricing is plausible only where a large employer pays, such as the KHNP consortium at Dukovany, or in remote locations.
- **Container cost per bed.** This is unpriced, so I make an assumption. If a 6 m obytný kontejner (CZK 4,000–4,800/month) sleeps 2–4 workers, the unit rent per bed is about CZK 1,000–2,400 per month. That is far below ubytovna prices of CZK 4,800–9,000 per bed-month. The spread would have to cover sanitary and kitchen modules, utilities, land, security and management, none of which are priced in my sources.
- **Purchase vs rent payback.** A new 6 m buňka at CZK 110,000–155,000 against rent of CZK 3,100–4,800/month pays back in about 23–50 months at 100% utilisation. At 80% that becomes about 29–63 months, and at 60% about 38–83 months, before transport, maintenance and refurbishment. Rental fleets therefore need high utilisation and long asset life (section 5).
- **Transport share of the customer's bill.** These are my calculations from the tariffs above, assuming 36 CZK/km counted both ways and CZK 1,600 handling per move:
  - 50 km site: delivery plus pickup ≈ CZK 10,400.
  - 3-month rental: rent CZK 14,400 (CZK 4,800/month), plus about CZK 1,000 cleaning. Logistics ≈ **40%** of the bill.
  - 12-month rental: rent CZK 48,000 (CZK 4,000/month). Logistics ≈ **17–18%**.
  - 150 km site: logistics ≈ CZK 24,800, about **62%** of a 3-month rental.
  - So moving the unit dominates the cost of short or distant deployments. That fits the founder's thesis, but the effect is concentrated in short rentals and remote sites.
- **Polish HDS rates** of PLN 4.5–6.5/km ≈ CZK 26–38/km, comparable to Czech per-km trucking.

## 5. Unit economics: utilisation, delivery & installation share, lease duration, lifespan, refurbishment, residual value

### Takeaway
Rental fleets typically run at 50–80% utilisation: WillScot 52–60% in 2025; ATCO, Black Diamond MSS and Modulaire (2021) 74–87%. Worker camps run at 50–70%: Target 51% in 2025, Black Diamond WFS 56–63%, Civeo about 45% by my rough calculation. Transport is large and low-margin. WillScot's D&I line was about 17% of 2025 revenue but earned only about 16–17% gross margin, fell 7% in 2025 on fewer activations and returns, then jumped 25% in Q2 2026. - **What that means.** D&I is about a sixth of revenue earning about a sixth in margin: low-margin and volatile with project starts, but it gates the high-margin lease revenue. Each activation and return also triggers "unit preparation" refurbishment cost. Lowering per-move cost or the number of moves (fewer truck trips, less crane time, faster set-up, less make-ready work) improves the incumbents' weakest line and the customer's cost of short rentals.
- **Idle and misplaced fleet.** Structural idle capacity of 20–50%, the 53,000-unit fleet pruning and 108 branch exits at WillScot, and Black Diamond calling about 78% "optimal" all suggest that much capacity is idle *in the wrong place*. Repositioning means empty-leg trucking. Matching demand to the nearest idle unit, or pooling the fleets of small Czech renters, could raise utilisation.
- **Civeo's mix.** On owned-village room nights, about 2.78 m × ~$76 ≈ $212 m, or about 46% of Civeo's $460 m Australian revenue. The rest is integrated services (catering and FM at clients' camps), a more asset-light model. In Canada, owned-lodge billings are about 1.55 m × $97 ≈ $150 m, about 84% of $178.6 m.
- **Rough occupancy.** Civeo: (2.78 m + 1.55 m) billed room-nights ÷ 365 ≈ 11,900 rooms per night, against about 26,500 rooms ≈ 45%. This is crude: the room count covers year-end and excludes US and other effects. Target's implied capacity is about 16,600 beds (8,466 ÷ 0.51).
- **Czech duration tiers** amortise the fixed cost of delivery, pickup and cleaning: the small renters' substitute for incumbents' minimum-term rules.

Snoozebox, a portable shipping-container hotel for events, failed on lumpy event revenue, losses and debt-funded capex. Katerra and Veev, two heavily funded modular startups, collapsed after over-expansion or loss of financing. ### Inferences
- **Snoozebox lesson.** Event-only demand is seasonal and lumpy: hundreds of rooms for a few weekends, then idle. Funding the fleet with debt means fixed financing costs against volatile utilisation. The pivot to cheaper-to-deploy units and institutional customers (hospitals, councils) came too late. A Czech startup should anchor on multi-month B2B or public contracts, such as construction workforce housing or flood relief, and treat events as fill-in demand.
- **Boxabl lesson.** About 846 units built against 335 delivered: the bottleneck is downstream (site preparation, permitting, foundations and utilities, customer financing, delivery) rather than factory output. This supports the founder's view that the "last mile" is the real inefficiency, and extends it beyond trucking to site work.
- **Katerra and Veev lesson.** Capital-intensive vertical integration, scaled before unit economics are proven, fails when funding tightens. Asset-light starts (renting or sub-renting from incumbents, partnering with KOMA or Containex dealers) reduce that risk.
- **Incumbent lesson.** Even large players run 50–80% utilisation and suffer contract or commodity shocks. Diversified demand (construction plus public plus defence plus events) and flexible, movable capacity are the defence.

## 7. Where incumbents are weak: complaints, lead times, availability, quality, transport cost, setup time

### Takeaway
Incumbents' weak points cluster around logistics and pricing. Delivery, pickup and installation is low-margin, volatile and fee-heavy, and US customers complain about dry-run fees, prepaid pickup, billing cycles and auto-debits. Czech pricing is opaque: Algeco quotes on request, transport is charged separately or "on request", and crane, handling and cleaning are add-ons. Supply also collapses in demand surges: Germany saw shortages and doubled prices in 2015 and again in 2022–23. Czech worker-housing demand from megaprojects such as Dukovany meets municipal resistance to permanent ubytovny, a niche for removable, relocatable villages. These are hypotheses, not validated customer research.

### Inferences
These are gap hypotheses for the Czech startup, derived from the findings above and not validated.
1. **Logistics-light units.** Units that ship several per truck (folding or flat-pack), self-erect without an HDS or heavy crane, and need little make-ready work. They would attack the cost line that makes up about 40–60% of a short or distant Czech rental and about 17% of the US leader's revenue at thin margins.
2. **All-in transparent pricing.** A posted monthly price that includes delivery, pickup, set-up and cleaning, against "price on request" from Algeco and per-km add-ons elsewhere, with no dry-run-style fees. This would be a differentiator in a market where customers cannot easily compare total cost.
3. **Utilisation and routing layer.** Pooling idle units across the many small Czech renters and dispatching the nearest available one to cut empty legs and repositioning. Incumbents structurally carry 20–50% idle fleet.
4. **Removable worker villages for megaprojects.** For Dukovany from 2029 and similar projects (energy, defence, infrastructure), where municipalities resist permanent ubytovny, a temporary camp that leaves no permanent hostel may be politically easier. Pricing would sit between the ubytovna range (CZK 135–600 per night) and the Civeo range (about CZK 2,100 per night) and would need employer-paid contracts.
5. **Surge and emergency capacity.** Pre-positioned, fast-deploy fleets for floods and refugee waves, when incumbents run short and prices double (Germany 2015). Public rental budgets such as Živel 4 (up to CZK 1 bn) favour rental over purchase.
