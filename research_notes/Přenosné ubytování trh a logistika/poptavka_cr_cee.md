## 1. Large projects and workforce peaks (Dukovany II, VRT, motorways and railways, energy, data centres and factories, defence): what is said about housing the workers?

### Takeaway
Dukovany II is by far the largest concentrated demand point in Czechia. EDU II/KHNP cite a peak of **~6,000 workers (2030–2034)**, and some EDU II statements say **up to ~10,000** at the peak overlap of the two units. The wider area has only **~7,500 temporary beds, of which just 23% are near the plant**. Worker accommodation is explicitly unresolved as of mid-2026: the Korean side plans closed "camps"/a campus, municipalities are rezoning to block new ubytovny, and Kraj Vysočina says housing locations must be settled before other investments. Other programmes (ŘSD's ~154 km of starts in 2026–27, the VRT slipping to ~2030, ČEPS's 80+ bn CZK grid plan, SMRs in the mid-to-late 2030s, AI data centres) are big but spread out, and none of the sources found says anything about how their workers will be housed.

### Inferences
- **The Dukovany bed gap is large and time-boxed.** Peak workforce is 6,000–10,000 in 2030–2034, against ~1,700 temporary beds near the plant (23% × 7,500) and 250 beds in Dukovany village. Assume, without a source, that 30–50% of the peak workforce commutes daily. The temporary-bed need at peak is then roughly **3,000–7,000 beds for ~4–6 years**, with ramp-up from 2029 and run-down around 2035–2037.
- Municipalities are blocking permanent ubytovny through územní plány. That favours **removable, campus-style modular accommodation** that can be dismantled after the peak, which is exactly what Daewoo/KHNP plan. The same zoning tools could also block temporary camps (the Jeseník mobilheim case shows this; see Q2), so **permitting know-how and municipal agreements** are part of the product.
- The criticised per-person space difference in the Daewoo concept suggests that **standard and dignity** (m² per person, sanitation ratio) will be politically scrutinised. A Czech supplier that offers a compliant, "hygiene-rule-proof" standard may have an edge.
- Linear projects (ŘSD ~154 km of starts in 2026–27, ČEPS line rebuilds, SŽ modernisations) produce **many dispersed, moving sites** of 15–20 km lasting 2–4 years. That suits relocatable crew camps or units, but no source quantifies workforce per section.
- VRT and SMR demand largely falls **after 2030** (VRT main sections ~2030+, Temelín SMR mid/late-2030s). Dukovany (2029–2036) is the anchor of the 2026–2035 window, followed by motorways and the grid.
- In Poland, the plant owner provides a dedicated on-site complex for only ~10% of workers, with the rest in the surrounding market. A similar split at Dukovany would push most demand onto a regional market that has few beds near the plant.

- D3, D49 and D4 PPP: no 2026-specific status found in these searches.
- Dlouhé Stráně II and other pumped-storage projects: no construction start found.

- Defence: no specific barracks or infrastructure build projects with worker counts. The 154 vs 184 bn CZK budget conflict is unresolved.
- Dukovany: the final camp location, bed capacity and m²/person of the Daewoo concept were not found. The text of MMR action plan sections 4.1.2 and 4.2 was not retrieved (fetch failed).

## 2. Crisis housing: the 2024 floods, the 2022 refugee wave, and whether the state holds reserves of mobile housing

### Takeaway
The September 2024 floods affected ~20,000 households (MMR estimate). Long-term homelessness was much smaller: ~100 demolitions were recommended in MSK alone, Živel 3 drew >4,700 rebuild applications, and ~130 people were still in Krnov evacuation centres days after the flood. The state funded **rental of modular or mobile homes through Živel 4 (up to 1 bn CZK)**, but uptake is undocumented and **mobilheimy in Jeseník had to go because územní plány don't allow them**. In 2022 the refugee response relied on halls, dormitories and tent camps (16 beds per tent, 30-day maximum), and capacity ran out (state: spring 2022; Prague: Nov 2022). **No evidence was found that SSHR holds mobile housing units.** What its 2024 flood deliveries document is dehumidifiers, generators, water tankers, tents and blankets.

- **[FACT]** Frankenthal: 9 container installations for €6.44m gross, rented until 31 Aug 2026 with an option of 2×6 months — [Frankenthal Ratsinfo](https://bis.frankenthal.de/oparl/bodies/0001/downloadfiles/a/00086957.pdf). Ludwigshafen: €3.15m one-off plus €290k per year for a container village — [Rheinpfalz](https://www.rheinpfalz.de/lokal/ludwigshafen_artikel,-stadt-baut-containerdorf-f%C3%BCr-fl%C3%BCchtlinge-_arid,86256.html)
- **[CLAIM]** Elmshorn: 128 men housed for €2.5m per year, with the containers themselves rentable for ~1/5 of that (opinionated source) — [Tichys Einblick](https://www.tichyseinblick.de/daili-es-sentials/unterbringung-gefluechtete-kommunen-kosten/)

### Inferences
- **Flood-displacement scale** is **hundreds to low thousands of households** needing medium-term (6–24 month) housing: ~100+ demolitions in MSK, >4,700 Živel 3 rebuild applications, plus the "people from demolitions" ČvT flags as most in need. It is not tens of thousands. The need is concentrated in small municipalities (Jesenicko, Krnovsko, Opavsko), where there is no rental market.
- **Funding exists, delivery and permitting don't.** Živel 4 put up to 1 bn CZK on the table for renting modular or mobile homes. Yet the only documented outcome is a handful of privately bought mobilheimy that were then ordered removed under the územní plán, plus modular schools. That points to **no ready rental fleet and no pre-agreed legal path**: the temporary-structure permit, its duration and the site.
- **No national mobile-housing stockpile was found.** SSHR's documented 2024 role was tents, blankets, dehumidifiers, generators and water. A "fleet-as-a-service" (framework contract with HZS, kraje or MMR for N winterised units deployable within X days) appears to be an **unoccupied niche**; this is an inference to validate.
- **The 2022 lessons point to winter-capable units.** Tents (16 beds, 30-day maximum) and halls were stopgaps used through summer only, and Prague ran out of capacity by November 2022. The temporary-protection reform taking effect on 1 Jan 2027 could create a smaller new wave of housing need among Ukrainians who lose status or support (speculative).
- **Price benchmark.** German municipal container shelters cost ~€62 per place per day all-in, including care and security. Container hardware rental alone is plausibly ~€300–350 per place per month (Elmshorn, opinionated source: €500k/128/12). That is the order of magnitude a Czech crisis-unit rental model would face.

## 3. Seasonal and foreign workers: agriculture, tourism, events; foreign workers in construction; Režim Ukrajina and quotas

### Takeaway
Czechia had **1.13 million registered foreigners** at the end of 2025, about **358,000 employed Ukrainians** and ~**15,000 Filipinos** (doubled in under two years). Construction employs ~415,000 people, ~40,000 of them Ukrainians, and a gap of up to 75,000 workers is forecast. Third-country labour migration runs mainly through employee cards (92%) and government programmes; the Filipino quota is **10,300 a year** and is pressured upward. Seasonal agriculture is small and short (hop growers take ~2,000–3,000 seasonal workers a year, an older figure). Events are showing early demand for portable accommodation: **Colours of Ostrava 2026 had its first container hotel (~20 four-person units)**, its camp sold out, and hotels raised prices up to 5×.

- **The structural demand driver is the foreign workforce, not seasonality.** Some 450k+ third-country workers (2024) and 358k Ukrainian employees (2025) mostly need **long-stay, low-cost beds**, today largely met by ubytovny and agency-bundled housing (see Q4). Construction alone has ~40k Ukrainians plus growing Filipino and Central Asian inflows, and a projected 75k gap implies more imported workers who need housing.
- **Quota expansion is a leading indicator.** A higher Filipino quota (from 2027?) and any new programmes would add thousands of workers per year who arrive **without housing**. Employers must arrange accommodation, and the 50k CZK sanction regime makes that a compliance item.
- **Seasonal agriculture is a small, spiky niche.** Hops need ~2–3k workers for a few weeks (Aug–Sep, older figure), plus fruit, vegetables and vineyards. A rental fleet could serve it in the off-season of construction (winter) only if logistics work, but the peaks overlap with the construction season.
- **Events are a proven but small premium niche.** Colours used ~80 container beds in 2026, a first. Price-gouging (5×) and sold-out camps show willingness to pay. Rotating a fleet between festivals (June–August) and crisis or worker use (rest of year) is a possible utilisation strategy (inference).

## 4. Current practice and prices: how workers are housed, ubytovna Kč/bed-night, shortages near big projects, media reports, municipal attitudes

### Takeaway
Worker ubytovny advertise **~135–600 Kč per bed-night**: typically **150–350 Kč** for long stays in shared rooms and 400–600 Kč for short stays, with steep discounts for 15–30+ nights. That is roughly **4,500–10,500 Kč per bed per month**. Supply is dense in industrial and agency hotspots (e.g., Tachov ORP: ~17% foreign residents, ~800 accommodation facilities) but thin near Dukovany. Documented problems include agency–ubytovna "business" exploiting Ukrainians, illegal work concentrated in construction, police checks of ubytovny, and municipalities wanting powers against disorderly foreign residents and rezoning to block new ubytovny.

### Inferences
- **Price ceiling for a worker-camp offer.** Where ubytovny exist, the market rate is ~150–350 Kč per bed-night for long stays, or ~4,500–10,500 Kč per month. A portable or modular product competes on **location and availability, not price**: remote sites such as Dukovany, motorway sections in rural areas, and flood-hit villages. In ubytovna-dense regions (Most, Kladno, Tachov, Mladá Boleslav) it would struggle on price.
- **Rough unit-economics check (assumption-laden).** Container rental alone in DE is ~€325 per place per month (Elmshorn: €500k/128/12), which at an assumed ~25 CZK/EUR is ~8,000 Kč per month. That is already at the upper end of Czech ubytovna prices before operations, utilities and cleaning. A viable offer likely needs **higher utilisation, multi-bed units, owned rather than rented hardware, or a premium for proximity, speed and compliance.**
- **Reputational and regulatory tailwind for "compliant" housing.** Exploitation scandals (agency–ubytovna chains), SÚIP enforcement, police checks, municipal pushback and the 2027 Filipino quota changes all push large employers and general contractors toward **transparent, standard-compliant, employer-controlled housing**. That is an angle for a professional operator, as opposed to informal ubytovny.
- **Municipal opposition is to permanent ubytovny and uncontrolled inflows**, not explicitly to temporary camps. A product that comes with a fixed end date, removal guarantee, site management and security might be acceptable where ubytovny are rezoned out. This is untested; the Jeseník case shows územní plán barriers apply even to temporary units.

### Gaps
