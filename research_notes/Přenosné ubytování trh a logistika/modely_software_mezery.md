Research date: 27 Oct 2026. Method note: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through, and every WebFetch attempt failed with DNS errors (forbes.cz, cc.cz, cez.cz, eastsuffolk.gov.uk, boden.se, lodgelink.com, hotelfriend.com). ## 1. Workforce-housing marketplaces and booking platforms: who they are, how they make money, what scale

### Takeaway
Two platform archetypes dominate. The first is managed B2B "crew travel" platforms that take the booking, pay the property and send the employer one invoice: LodgeLink (Civeo; Canada, US and Australia), Roomex (Europe, owned by FLEETCOR) and Engine (US). The second is listing directories where the company or worker deals directly with the landlord and the landlord pays to be listed: Monteurzimmer.de/.at (Funke) and the older mein-monteurzimmer.de. In CZ, SK and PL, demand is still met through classifieds and directories (firmy.cz, OLX, nehnutelnosti.sk). The one venture-backed Czech attempt, Accomango (2020–21), raised money for a SaaS/marketplace for worker housing, then laid off all staff and pivoted to buying ubytovny itself.

### Inferences
- [Assessment] Monetisation spectrum, from lowest to highest take:
  1. Listing or subscription paid by the landlord, with direct booking (Monteurzimmer-type; mein-monteurzimmer 2015).
  2. Transaction fee or margin on managed bookings with consolidated invoicing and payment float (Engine, Roomex, LodgeLink).
  3. Full-service per-bed-night contracts with minimum commitments (Civeo/Target; see section 3).
  Revenue per unit rises sharply moving from (1) to (3), and so does the operating burden.
- [Assessment] LodgeLink works because Civeo already owns lodges and crew relationships, so the marketplace sits on top of an operating business. Civeo's Australian business made up about 73% of FY2025 revenue (460.3/630.8, my calculation). This is evidence for a "software on top of operations" model rather than a pure marketplace.
- [Assessment] I found no managed, LodgeLink-type crew-lodging platform focused on CZ, SK, PL or AT. DE/AT has listing directories (Monteurzimmer). Roomex (Dublin) targets European construction and field-service firms, but its actual CEE coverage was not verified. CZ/PL demand runs through classifieds (OLX, firmy.cz) and personal networks of agencies.
- [Assessment] Accomango's history (2021 raise → layoffs → buying ubytovny) is the most relevant local precedent. A pure software/marketplace layer for Czech worker housing did not gain traction in 2021–22, and the founders themselves concluded that controlling supply (operations) was a prerequisite.
- [Assessment] Price anchors are very low (about 30 zł, roughly €7, per bed-night in PL classifieds). Any commission model on CEE worker beds yields small absolute revenue per booking, which pushes toward volume contracts, consolidated invoicing and operations fees.

### Gaps
- No LodgeLink commission %, GMV or revenue figures were found. Civeo's Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript exists ([Webull](https://www.webull.com/news/15319458561795072)) but could not be read.
- Monteurzimmer.de's current landlord pricing (subscription vs commission) and inventory size were not found. The only figures come from AI-generated bau.de content.
- No dedicated Polish B2B worker-housing platform was identified (only OLX). Czech "ubytování pro montéry" portals and German corporate-housing platforms (e.g., Homelike, Wunderflats) could not be checked after the search budget ran out.
- Accomango's current status (post-pivot), the date of the pivot article and the number of ubytovny it bought are unknown.
- Flatio's revenue, take rate and B2B share are unknown.

## 2. Camp / worker-accommodation management software, and whether any of it automates compliance (e.g., foreign-guest reporting)

### Takeaway
Specialised "camp management" software is dominated by Australian and North American vendors (ResortData, RMS, INX, CampWare, IBS iLogistics). These products focus on bed allocation, arrivals and departures, FIFO rosters and flights, housekeeping, and reconciling occupancy against payroll or contractor records. In Europe the closest offer is HotelFriend (Berlin), a hotel PMS marketed to Monteur/worker-accommodation providers. I found no CEE-native camp or ubytovna operations product. No evidence surfaced that any camp tool integrates Czech Ubyport, the planned eTurista system, or German ArbStättV accommodation documentation, even though these obligations are concrete and enforced.

### Cited Findings
#### Product landscape
- [Company PR] ResortData sells "workforce housing" property-management software for workforce camps and staff accommodation. It supports regrouping and reassigning staff and tracks bed rentals and occupancy against payroll or contractor records. — [ResortData workforce housing](https://resortdata.com/workforce-housing/)
- [Company PR / exhibitor profile] RMS calls itself "a leading global workforce accommodation management platform" for energy producers and camp operators. It covers room allocation, arrivals and departures, housekeeping coordination, billing and reporting, plus contractor-stay management and "real-time insights for room usage and compliance". I found no evidence of a rostering module. — [Australian Energy Producers Conference exhibitor page](https://energyproducersconference.au/exhibition/exhibitors/?view=1299)
- [Company PR] INX (Australia) markets workforce management in which InFlight manages "your roster, flight and accommodation requirements". — [INX workforce management](https://inxsoftware.com/workforce-management). Cru Software and IONYX/Fly2Work sell standalone FIFO rostering. — [Cru Software (MINE Australia, Jun 2024)](https://mine.nridigital.com/mine_australia_jun24/cru_software_company_insight); [IONYX Fly2Work](https://www.mining-technology.com/contractors/resource/ionyx/pressreleases/fly2work-roster-expansion/)
- [Vendor listing] IBS Software's iLogistics Accommodation Management is a cloud product for oil & gas and mining. It tracks personnel arrivals and departures and integrates with RTLS/RFID badges for automated tracking (access-control-style presence). — [SoftwareOne marketplace listing](https://platform.softwareone.com/product/ilogistics-accommodation-management/PCP-7864-3142)

#### Compliance obligations that a worker-housing tool would need to automate
- [Official] **CZ:** The accommodation provider must report a foreigner's stay to the Foreign Police (via Ubyport) within 3 working days and keep a domovní kniha (house register) for police inspection. The police FAQ also lists accommodation standards, including that it must not be obviously inferior to comparable local providers and a minimum of 8 m² for one person and 12.6 m² for two. — [Policie ČR FAQ](https://policie.gov.cz/reditelstvi-sluzby-cizinecke-policie/casto-kladene-otazky); [Policie ČR hlášení pobytu cizinců](https://policie.gov.cz/reditelstvi-sluzby-cizinecke-policie/hlaseni-pobytu-cizincu)
- [Official] **CZ enforcement:** The "ŠTÍT 2025" campaign targets accommodation facilities. In Liberec region, operations on 19 and 26 Oct 2025 found 20 violations by foreigners and led to 7 people being detained. — [Policie ČR Liberec](https://policie.gov.cz/clanek/pobytove-kontroly-cizincu-v-libereckem-kraji-pokracuji.aspx); [Policie ČR Hradec Králové checks](https://policie.gov.cz/kralovehradecky-kraj/kontroly-ubytovacich-zarizeni-v-hradci-kralove)
- [Vendor blog] **CZ eTurista:** The system is meant to replace Ubyport and paper guest books and to link guest reporting with municipalities, foreign police and the tax authority. The Ministry for Regional Development (MMR) is developing it, but the enabling amendment to the tourism act had not been definitively approved and the launch keeps being postponed, so Ubyport (3 working days) still applies. — [Chekin CZ blog](https://chekin.com/cs/blog/ubyport-a-e-turista-jak-splnit-povinnosti-evidence-hostu/)
- [Press] eTurista was originally meant to apply from January 2026. Critics object to central storage of data on millions of guests with broad state access, and parliamentary opposition is growing. — [echo24](https://www.echo24.cz/a/Hcpik/zpravy-domaci-registr-turistu-eturista-evidence-ubytovani-osobni-udaje-soukromi); [echo24 (parliament)](https://www.echo24.cz/a/HBKWi/zpravy-domov-registr-kde-jste-spali-stat-smirovani-turiste-odpor-parlament)
- [Press] e15 reports municipalities could gain "up to 5 bn CZK per year" from better collection of local fees and taxes via eTurista; the stated start ("July of next year") is undated. — [e15](https://www.e15.cz/byznys/obchod-a-sluzby/az-pet-miliard-rocne-pro-obce-navic-system-eturista-zlepsi-vyber-mistnich-poplatku-a-dani-1426093). A debate about whether flats used for accommodation need kolaudace (building-use approval) as accommodation facilities is reported by [finance.cz](https://www.finance.cz/clanky/musi-mit-byt-kolaudaci-jako-ubytovaci-zarizeni-debata-o-e-turistovi-se-vyostruje/).
- [Official] **DE:** Under the ArbStättV, a Gemeinschaftsunterkunft is housing provided by the employer, or a third party on its behalf, on or off a site, paid or free, and shared by at least 4 people. Before providing it, the employer must carry out and document a Gefährdungsbeurteilung (§3). The employer must also document the addresses, capacities, the Zuordnung (allocation) of each worker and the accommodation period. This documentation must be available at the place of work from day one and kept for 4 weeks after the stay ends. ASR A4.4 "Unterkünfte" sets the details. — [NRW Verwaltungsvorschrift "Überprüfung der Unterkünfte von Beschäftigten" (2022)](https://recht.nrw.de/lrmb/verwaltungsvorschrift/21102022-ueberpruefung-der-unterkuenfte-von-beschaeftigten/); [BG BAU, 2021](https://www.bgbau.de/fileadmin/Medien-Objekte/Medien/Sonstige_Medien/20210205_Neue_Anforderungen_an_Gemeinschaftsunterk%C3%BCnfte__002_.pdf); [KomNet NRW](https://www.komnet.nrw.de/_sitetools/dialog/1692)
- [Union document, 2020] The NGG union's 2020 text on the Arbeitsschutzkontrollgesetz described the new accommodation rules, including a reporting duty to state authorities. Whether that reporting duty took effect could not be verified. — [NGG 2020 PDF](https://www.ngg.net/fileadmin/Hauptverwaltung/Redaktion_Webseite_ngg.net/Fleisch-Unterseiten_ngg.net/Fleisch-Initiativen-Konferenz/Unterbringung_im_Arbeitsschutzkontrollgesetz__in_endg._Fassung_141220.pdf)

### Inferences
- [Assessment] The established camp suites (ResortData, RMS, INX, CampWare, iLogistics) are built for large remote camps with FIFO rosters, catering and flights, mainly in Australia and North America. I found no evidence of CEE localisation such as Czech/Polish language, Ubyport, domovní kniha, poplatek z pobytu or ArbStättV exports.
- [Assessment] CEE worker housing is mostly many small sites (ubytovny, flats, pensions) used by agencies and contractors. Its real job is less "camp catering and rosters" and more bed-level allocation across dispersed sites, combined with compliance records (Ubyport within 3 working days, domovní kniha, 8 m² rule, ArbStättV Zuordnung for crews posted to DE), keys, damage documentation and consolidated billing or payroll deductions. This maps closely onto BULO's existing modules: resident records, self-service keys, photo defect reporting, technician workflow, payments and building passport.
- [Assessment] Regulatory momentum (ŠTÍT 2025 checks, the possible eTurista transition, DE documentation duties) creates a "must-do" compliance driver. The risk is that eTurista is delayed or politically contested.
- [Assessment] Guest-registration automation vendors (e.g., Chekin, which publishes Czech Ubyport/eTurista guidance) already target hotel and short-term-rental compliance. A worker-housing product would need to differentiate on multi-site, employer-facing features (allocation, Zuordnung reports, recharges) rather than on registration alone.

### Gaps
- I could not verify whether Czech PMS vendors (Previo, HORES, Mews, Hotel Time) or HotelFriend integrate Ubyport or German Meldeschein/ArbStättV exports.
- No source confirmed meal-management, access-control or rotation features for ResortData, RMS or CampWare beyond the snippets above.
- No CEE-native ubytovna management software was identified. Searches for "software pro ubytovny" and Polish equivalents (e.g., KWHotel) could not be run after the budget ran out.
- I did not cover the Dutch SNF labour-migrant housing certification, a possible compliance analogue.

## 3. Capacity-as-a-service, full-service contracts, temporary-to-permanent ("legacy") models, and seasonal reuse

### Takeaway
Full-service operators sell beds-plus-services under multi-year contracts with take-or-pay or committed-minimum-revenue terms: Civeo's Bowen Basin take-or-pay contracts, and Target Hospitality's Workforce Hub contract with about US$111M committed minimum out of about US$175M expected. Megaproject accommodation strategies (Hinkley Point C, Sizewell C) show that purpose-built campuses house only a minority of workers. The rest is absorbed by the local market, holiday parks and housing-fund schemes that activate latent beds. "Legacy" conversion works when it is designed in from the start (Milano-Cortina village → 1,700 student beds). It fails when campuses are modular and temporary (Hinkley's Bridgwater campus will be dismantled).

### Cited Findings
#### Per-bed / full-service contracts with minimum commitments

The deal was expected to add about US$32M of annualised revenue and US$17M of adjusted EBITDA, and the acquired contracts include take-or-pay contracts with metallurgical-coal producers. — [Business Wire, Feb 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250219579506/en); [Business Wire, completion, May 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250507511630/en)
- [Press] The same deal was reported as "mining dongas sold at A$78,000 per bedroom". — [realcommercial.com.au](https://www.realcommercial.com.au/news/mining-dongas-sold-at-78000-per-bedroom-to-us-firm)
- [Company PR] In Q2 2025, Civeo won a four-year contract at its owned Bowen Basin villages (expected revenue A$250M) and a three-year integrated-services contract (A$64M). — [Civeo Q2 2025 results](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250729679536/en/Civeo-Reports-Second-Quarter-2025-Results/); [MarketBeat call transcript](https://www.marketbeat.com/earnings/reports/2025-7-29-civeo-co-stock)

### Inferences
- [Assessment] Commitment terms are the core of the full-service model. Target's committed minimum is about 63% of expected contract value (111.1/175.2, my calculation). - [Assessment] Indicative unit economics: Civeo paid about A$78k per operating room (A$105M/1,340, matching the press figure). Expected annualised revenue of about US$32M over 1,340 rooms is about US$24k per room per year, roughly US$65 per room-night all-in at full occupancy (my rough calculation; actual occupancy and service mix unknown). ## 4. Circular and second-life models for modular units (buy-back, refurbishment, used leasing, secondary marketplaces)

### Takeaway
Rental fleets already run a circular model: rent, return, recondition and redeploy (Algeco, Adapteo). Containex offers a formal but restricted buy-back where the seller bears dismantling and transport. Units are being moved between projects in practice (Stegra reused prefabricated modules from Skellefteå in Boden). Secondary marketplaces exist for shipping containers (Container xChange), but I found no equivalent for accommodation modules. Used modules are sold through the manufacturers' own used-sales pages and general classifieds.

### Inferences
- [Assessment] Second life is already captured inside the big rental fleets (Algeco, Containex, Adapteo, KOMA RENT), which control refurbishment and redeployment. The open gaps are (i) units owned outside these fleets (contractors, agencies, municipalities, camp owners), which lack a structured resale or redeployment channel, and (ii) condition and specification transparency. Containex's buy-back only accepts units in original specification, and the seller pays dismantling and transport. A per-unit digital passport (condition, repairs, inspections, utility interfaces, relocation history) would reduce friction in buy-back, resale and redeployment. This is a direct extension of BULO's digital building passport.
- [Assessment] The Stegra/Skellefteå case shows cross-project redeployment of accommodation modules happens and is cost-relevant at Nordic megaprojects. A comparable flow is plausible in CEE as projects peak and wind down (PEJ, Paks II, Dukovany II; see section 5).
- [Assessment] Container xChange shows that a neutral marketplace can work when the asset is standardised (ISO containers), flows are global and liquid, and trust is the friction. Accommodation modules are less standardised and lower-volume, so a pure marketplace is weaker. A fleet-agnostic "redeployment desk" with passports and logistics coordination is more plausible.

### Gaps
- No CEE secondary marketplace dedicated to used accommodation or office containers was identified. Bazos/Sbazar/OLX volumes, and price depreciation curves for used modules, were not researched.
- Algeco CZ and Containex CZ rental-fleet sizes, utilisation and buy-back uptake are unknown. No Algeco buy-back terms were found.
- No data was found on how many times a typical module is relocated over its life (Adapteo gives no figure).

## 5. Candidate white spaces for a CZ/CEE software-plus-operations startup: evidence, competitors, barriers, fit

### Takeaway
[Assessment] The strongest gap is an operating and compliance layer for dispersed worker beds. It would let an employer, agency or megaproject treat modular camps, ubytovny, flats and holiday parks as one managed, compliant inventory: bed-level allocation, Ubyport/ArbStättV records, keys, damage documentation, maintenance, consolidated billing and shuttle coordination. It would be anchored on large demand nodes (Dukovany II about 10,000 peak workers, PEJ about 12,000, Paks II 8,000–10,000) and extended later into module passports for relocation and resale. Abroad, LodgeLink/Civeo proves the demand-side model. In CEE nothing equivalent was found, and Accomango's 2021–22 failure shows the software must come bundled with operations and anchor contracts. Repositioning marketplaces (c) and utility pods (d) are real needs but weaker standalone businesses for a software company. Legacy partnerships (e) are a strong policy hook around Dukovany but capital-heavy.

### Inferences
- [Assessment] Sizing (my calculations from the cited figures):
  - Dukovany II: about 10,000 peak workers vs 7,573 existing temporary beds in the region. Even using every existing bed would leave a shortfall of at least about 2,400, before tourism and other demand is counted.
  - PEJ: about 1,000 of about 12,000 peak workers in an on-site base, so roughly 90%+ must be housed and transported within about 45 minutes.
  - Paks II: 8,000–10,000 at peak.
  - Together these three CEE nuclear builds imply roughly 30,000 peak workers needing housing over the coming decade. Peak timing and any overlap between projects are not verified; the only date captured is PEJ's 2036 first power per Forsal. The baseline market is about 845k foreign employment relationships in CZ, a large share in agency or ubytovna housing (share not quantified; see Gaps).

- [Assessment] Additional gaps beyond (a)–(e):
  - **(f) Per-unit "module passport" for relocatable buildings** (condition, inspections, fire and hygiene documents, utility interface specs, relocation history). It enables buy-back, resale and redeployment, which Containex restricts to original-spec units, and supports quality claims. It is closest to BULO's core and connects (c), (d) and (e).
  - **(g) Housing-fund and local-bed activation administration** for developers and municipalities: lodging schemes, empty-home reactivation, rent support, provider databases. HPC and Sizewell run these via councils, and PEJ compiled databases by hand. This could be a B2G/B2B SaaS-plus-service around Dukovany's compensation funds.
  - **(h) Worker-housing quality audit and certification**, given the conditions reported at Płock, police checks and the IFC/EBRD standards. It could be bundled with (b) as a "certified bed" label that EPCs can require in subcontracts.
- [Assessment] On the founder's goal of "adding capacity quickly where needed": the fastest lever shown in the evidence is not trucking speed. It is (1) pre-contracted access to rental fleets and reusable modules (Stegra/Boden planned 712 beds from reused Skellefteå modules, with occupancy targeted for late autumn 2025, about 5 months after the June 2025 announcement; delivery not verified), (2) activating existing beds (Sizewell's Pontins chalets with buses; HPC's lodgings and empty-homes schemes), and (3) standardised utility hookups. Software adds the most value in coordinating these three, not in a freight marketplace alone.
- [Assessment] Main risks:
  - Thin unit prices in CEE (about €7 per bed-night in PL classifieds).
  - Customer concentration in a few megaprojects.
  - eTurista or migration-policy shifts.
  - Accomango's evidence that operators do not adopt software unless it comes with demand (filled beds) or removes a legal risk.
